- First group English pulses into thought units. Translate each WHOLE unit as one natural sentence (holistic mapping), then distribute it over its pulses; never map pulse-by-pulse.
- Case 1, one thought over several pulses: put " | " at each real gap. Each segment carries the SAME words/ideas as its English pulse, in the English order. Only the minimum may cross a " | " (in SOV languages, the verb moving to the end of its clause) — never move a name, number, key noun, example or clause to another pulse.
- Case 2, several thoughts in one continuous pulse: no " | "; use "," between rapid thought units, ";" between different thoughts.
- Case 3, a pulse ends thought A and starts B: normal boundary punctuation; " | " or "-" only at the real gap.
- Never invent a pause or smooth over a real one. Keep the order of thought units, clauses and key words as in English (no clause flipping) — the listener must hear each idea when the speaker says it.
- "-" marks asides, a list intro, added explanation after a completed thought, a phrase defining the item just named, abrupt breaks.
- Integrity: never add or drop meaning (no invented not/only/even/never). Keep every repetition, aside, detail, name, number, other speakers. Leave tags untouched. No commentary. Match tone; questions stay short, spoken.
- Review: 1) express the intention, not the literal words — but in the English order; 2) use the context-specific word, not the generic one; 3) direct quotes/inner monologue: subject, "quote", then the say/think verb at the end; 4) long multi-part sentences: add small anchor words tying sub-parts to the main idea; 5) fix broken flow by reordering only the smallest blocks; 6) syllable economy: fit the English segment's length (spoken contractions, drop repeated pronouns, empty fillers); shorten wording, never content.
